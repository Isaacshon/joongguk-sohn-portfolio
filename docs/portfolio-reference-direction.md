# Portfolio reference direction

Reviewed: 5 September 2026. This is the editorial brief for the portfolio upgrade, not a declaration that every proposed interaction has already shipped.

## How the references are used

Each of the 28 projects has **one primary anchor**, recorded in `src/lib/project-design-references.ts`. Existing factual and historical sources remain supporting research. A primary anchor is a compositional and behavioural reference, not permission to copy the studio's artwork, layout verbatim, identity, proprietary font, or campaign photography.

The registry deliberately separates `observedPrinciple` (a concise paraphrase supported by the source) from `layoutDirection` and `interactionDirection` (our independent adaptation). Nothing here implies that a named studio or brand commissioned, approved, or collaborated on the portfolio project. Brand projects retain the small fictional-project disclosure at their foot.

The main agent is using Chrome for visual research. This registry adds exact studio and brand source pages verified through web research, so references do not stop at unattributed Pinterest images. Seasonal retail pages are volatile: ZARA uses its stable official SS25 campaign PDF as the primary anchor, not a redirected SS26 store link. The UNIQLO LifeWear page is an enduring brand explanation, not a claim to be its newest issue.

## Project-detail direction and scope boundary

- Preserve the existing portfolio home, Work index, navigation, and overall site identity. The user explicitly limited redesign to individual project pages; earlier home/index redesign directions are withdrawn.
- Within each project detail, the opening viewport should offer meaningful project imagery and a clear route through that case study. Avoid imposing a universal beige world or decorative taxonomy across projects.
- Present a world through visual evidence. A large claim followed by a small image is not automatically editorial design.
- Every project needs one recognisable behaviour tied to its subject. Registration, inspection, tuning, route navigation, repair comparison, and room selection are different actions, not renamed scroll reveals.
- On mobile, preserve the action and the narrative. Use an accessible selector, image strip, or compact map where appropriate; do not simply collapse every desktop column into the same stack.
- Keep captions in normal document flow. A selected image may be immersive; its supporting text must not overlap another image, spill across a column, or require clipping to fit.
- Typography follows the amount of information and the available width. Use phrase-level wrapping and controlled measures, not a manual line break after each word. Paragraphs remain comfortably readable.
- Strong design also includes restraint: reduced motion, keyboard operation, visible focus, meaningful controls, no compulsory audio, and no scroll trapping.

## Review gates

1. The first image and opening typography must belong to the same world.
2. The primary interaction must reveal, compare, navigate, or explain actual project material.
3. Mobile must retain that action with a visible non-gesture alternative.
4. All eight personal-project photographs must remain accessible without broken images or hidden required content.
5. No heading or caption may overlap, clip, or create horizontal page overflow at 390, 768, and 1440 pixels.
6. Selection, focus, back navigation, motion preference, and close controls must be tested, not merely styled.
7. A short source credit may live at the foot of a case study. Research prose should not become another oversized block in the visual story.
